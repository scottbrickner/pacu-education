The patient
01

The patient

CORE

Why this swan is staying in, and why it matters for how you land it.

These aren't fresh open-heart patients. They came for a diagnostic right heart cath, and the team kept the catheter in because the numbers are going to drive therapy for days — sometimes weeks when the patient is being listed for heart transplant. Your job in PACU is to protect the catheter, protect the data, and protect the patient until they reach 6EW.

Population 1

Advanced HF · tailored therapy

Inotropes, diuretics, and afterload reducers titrated to filling pressures and cardiac output.

  • Watch: RA, PA diastolic, CO/CI, SvO₂
  • Often on milrinone or dobutamine
  • Low-output signs matter more than one number

Population 2

Transplant / LVAD evaluation

Is the pulmonary vasculature and right ventricle ready for a new heart or a pump?

  • Watch: PVR, TPG, RA:PAWP, PAPi
  • May have had vasoreactivity testing (nitroprusside, milrinone, iNO)
  • RV function is the headline

Population 3

Pulmonary hypertension

PAH therapy titration and vasodilator response.

  • Watch: mPAP, PVR, RA, CO
  • Never interrupt a continuous prostacyclin infusion (epoprostenol, treprostinil)
  • Catheter whip is common; PA rupture risk is higher
Keck · CP 1-111

A PA thermodilution catheter is an intensity-of-service criterion for stepdown or ICU. Monitor and alarms must be directly seen or heard by staff at all times. These patients do not go to a general floor with a swan in.

Long dwell = more risk

Transplant-listing patients may keep this catheter for weeks, so how it's landed in PACU sets up everything after. The catheter softens and can migrate (check the cm marking every time), the balloon fatigues (SCU 10-408 flags rupture risk after > 4 days of repeated inflations), and every day adds CLABSI risk — a clean occlusive dressing, a closed system, and sterile caps start here.

Equipment
02

The catheter

CORE

Five lumens, one introducer, and a cm marking you'll check every time you touch it.

Five lumens, six connections

Keck most commonly uses a 5-lumen catheter. Colors below; every hub is also labeled — read both.

PAP · yellow

Distal lumen, opens at the tip in the pulmonary artery. Connects to the PAP transducer with pressurized NS flush.

Monitored continuously. No infusions, no meds. Mixed venous gas is the only draw. Fast flush ≤ 2 sec.

CVP · blue

Proximal injectate lumen, opens ~30 cm from the tip in the right atrium. Connects to the CVP transducer and the CO injectate syringe.

Monitored. No vasoactives here (the CO bolus would push them). No blood, albumin, or TPN.

RAP · white

Proximal infusion lumen, opens in the right atrium.

Infusion only — not monitored.

RVP · gray

Opens along the shaft in the right ventricle when the tip is in the PA. Used as a secondary infusion port.

Infusion only — not monitored.

Balloon · red

1.5 mL max. Gate valve: red lines aligned = open. Deflation is always passive — remove the syringe and let it go.

Use only the syringe that comes with the catheter (3 mL, fill ≤ 1.5 mL). Keep it attached, empty, and locked. Lost it? Don't substitute another syringe — escalate.

Thermistor connector

Senses blood temperature near the tip for thermodilution cardiac output. Connects to the CO cable/module.

Keep connected; a loose thermistor = no CO.

Two monitored, two for drips · cap every port

Only yellow (PAP) and blue (CVP) are transduced. White (RAP), gray (RVP), and the introducer (cordis) sidearm carry infusions and meds. Keep the sterile sleeve intact — no tape or dressing on the sleeve.

Red Curos · dead-enderEvery open port on the pressure tubing — PAP and CVP transducer stopcocks.
Green Curos · regularEvery infusion port with a luer-lock clave — white RAP, gray RVP, introducer sidearm.
First 15 minutes
03

Landing sequence

CORE

The first 15 minutes. Receive, connect, calibrate, confirm — in that order.

Receive BEDSIDE

  1. Bedside SBAR from cath lab RN / provider. Get the cm marking at the introducer.
  2. Get the balloon volume used and the final cath-lab numbers (RA, PA, PAWP, CO/CI, SvO₂).
  3. What infusions, through which lumen. Any heparin/anticoagulation given.
  4. Confirm the plan: targets, who titrates, and who to call.

Connect SET-UP

  1. Yellow → PAP transducer. Blue → CVP transducer + CO syringe. White (RAP) and gray (RVP) = infusions.
  2. 500 mL NS flush bag, pressure bag at 300 mmHg. Red dead-ender Curos on every pressure-tubing port; green Curos on every infusion clave.
  3. Tubing short and rigid (3–4 ft). All connections tight.
  4. Thermistor → CO cable. Infusions on white, gray, or the introducer sidearm.

Calibrate ACCURACY

  1. Mark the phlebostatic axis: 4th ICS, mid-AP chest.
  2. Level the air-zero stopcock to it with a carpenter level. Never eyeball.
  3. Zero: stopcock off to patient, open to air, press Zero, confirm, close to air.
  4. Square wave test. Document optimal / over / underdamped.

Confirm SAFETY

  1. PA waveform on the distal channel — not RV, not wedge.
  2. cm marking matches report. Balloon deflated, syringe attached, locked.
  3. PA alarms on, ±20% of baseline or per order. Never off.
  4. Site: occlusive dressing, no bleeding/hematoma. Lungs, SpO₂, rhythm. Position confirmation per order.
Your move

Re-level and re-zero with every position change and any time the numbers don't match the patient. A transducer 10 cm below the heart reads ~7 mmHg too high.

Troubleshooting
05

Recognize trouble

CORE

Keck troubleshooting steps from SCU 10-408, with a PACU default for anything outside current scope.

Spontaneous wedge

Act now

PA waveform flattens into a low, wedge-looking trace with no dicrotic notch. PAP alarm. Risk: pulmonary infarction or rupture.

  1. Rule out equipment: clot, air, kink.
  2. Check balloon: syringe off, red lines aligned, let it deflate passively.
  3. Have the patient cough or change position.
  4. Not resolved in 10 min: pull back 1 cm at a time, max 5 cm, until PA returns. Verify scope
  5. Still wedged: notify provider immediately.
PACU default until scope is setSteps 1–3, then call the provider / anesthesia right away. Do not reposition the catheter.

RV migration

Act now

Diastolic drops to near zero with a flat or rising diastolic slope — an RV trace on the distal channel. Often PVCs or runs of VT. cm marking has usually changed.

  1. Rule out equipment problems.
  2. Policy: inflate balloon to full capacity to float the tip back to PA; deflate as soon as PA returns. Verify scope
  3. Not back in 15 min: turn side to side; then notify provider.
  4. Ectopy with hemodynamic compromise: deflate balloon, withdraw to RA, notify. Verify scope
PACU default until scope is setKeep monitoring the distal waveform continuously, treat the rhythm, and notify the provider immediately.

Suspected balloon rupture

Act now

Blood back through the balloon port, no resistance on inflation, or no waveform change when inflated.

  1. Do not try to inflate again.
  2. Lock the port, remove the syringe, cap with a dead-ender.
  3. Tape over the port: "Ruptured balloon – do not inflate."
  4. Notify provider; expect imaging and/or removal.
Prevent itPassive deflation only. Never pull back on the balloon syringe.

Hemoptysis · PA rupture

Emergency

New blood in the airway, sudden hypoxemia, hypotension. Rare but often fatal. Higher risk: pulmonary hypertension, age > 60, anticoagulation, overinflated or distal tip.

  1. Call for help now: provider, anesthesia, rapid response.
  2. Leave the balloon alone unless a provider directs otherwise.
  3. If the bleeding side is known, position bleeding side down.
  4. Oxygen, airway support, prepare for ICU.
PACU noteThis is why PACU nurses don't touch the balloon routinely on this population.

Air embolism

Emergency

Sudden dyspnea, chest pain, hypotension, desaturation, altered LOC — especially after a disconnection or line change.

  1. Close off the source (clamp, stopcock, occlude).
  2. Left lateral, head down (Trendelenburg) if tolerated.
  3. 100% oxygen. Call provider / rapid response.
Prevent itLuer-lock everything, red/green Curos on every open port, patient exhales/breath-hold during any open-line step on IJ/subclavian lines.

Pneumothorax · hemothorax

Act now

IJ and subclavian insertions. Pleuritic chest pain, dyspnea, falling SpO₂, decreased breath sounds on the insertion side. Tracheal shift and hypotension are late.

  1. Assess lungs and SpO₂ on arrival and with any change.
  2. Notify provider; anticipate chest x-ray.
  3. Tension physiology (hypotension, rising airway pressure): rapid response.
PACU noteSubclavian bleeding is hard to compress — trend the site and the Hgb.

Overdamped waveform

Fix & watch

Rounded, sluggish trace, lost dicrotic notch, narrow pulse pressure. Numbers drift toward the mean.

  1. Pressure bag at 300 mmHg? Flush bag empty?
  2. Check every connection and stopcock position; look for kinks site-to-transducer.
  3. Suspect clot: aspirate first. Never flush against resistance.
  4. Remove air. Change the transducer if needed. Repeat square wave.
Watch forBlood, albumin, TPN, or lipids in the RA port — viscous fluids dampen and occlude.

Underdamped · catheter whip

Fix & watch

Spiky, erratic trace with overshoot; hard to find systolic and end-diastolic points. Common in pulmonary hypertension.

  1. Keep tubing to 3–4 ft; remove small bubbles.
  2. Agree with the provider on which systolic/diastolic points you'll report.
  3. Document and hand off the same points consistently.
  4. Persistent whip: ask provider to reposition.
TipEnd-diastole = 0.08 sec after QRS onset when whip isn't obscuring it.

Numbers don't fit the patient

Fix & watch

Pressures jumped after a position change, a bed move, or transfer onto the PACU monitor.

  1. Re-level to the phlebostatic axis with a level.
  2. Re-zero. Confirm the zero message.
  3. Square wave test. Read at end-expiration.
  4. Still off: change module/cable; Biomed to check with a simulator.
RememberDigital readings are fine unless respiratory variation is large (> 5 mmHg PA, > 3 mmHg RA) — then read from a strip.

No waveform · flat line

Fix & watch

Straight line on the PAP channel with no alarm cause obvious on the patient.

  1. Stopcock turned off to the patient?
  2. Cable seated? Module in? Power?
  3. Correct label/scale on the channel?
  4. Swap cable/module; call Biomed if unresolved.
Don't assumeLook at the patient first — then the equipment.

Site bleeding · hematoma

Fix & watch

Oozing through the dressing, neck swelling (IJ), or an expanding hematoma. Heparin in the cath lab raises the risk.

  1. Direct pressure where compressible; reinforce, then change dressing per CVAD policy.
  2. Neck swelling with airway change: rapid response.
  3. Notify provider; check anticoagulation and Hgb.
Also watchSigns of tamponade: hypotension, rising RA, narrowing pulse pressure.

New arrhythmias

Fix & watch

PVCs, runs of VT, or new atrial arrhythmias. Often mechanical irritation from the catheter.

  1. Check the distal waveform — RV trace means migration (see above).
  2. Check the cm marking against report.
  3. Treat per ACLS; notify provider.
Pacing wires in?Only a provider removes a PA line when transvenous or permanent wires are present.

What they're being titrated to

Common targets from the literature. Follow the provider's orders — goals are individualized.

Advanced HF

RA ≤ 8
  • PAWP ≤ 15–18 mmHg
  • CI ≥ 2.2 L/min/m²
  • SVR ~ 800–1200
  • SvO₂ ≥ 60–65%

"Warm and dry" without symptomatic hypotension.

Transplant / LVAD

PVR
  • PVR > 3 WU → vasoreactivity testing; persistently > 5 WU is a transplant red flag
  • TPG = mPAP − PAWP (> 12–15 concerning)
  • RA:PAWP > 0.63 and PAPi < 1.85 suggest RV dysfunction

Pulmonary hypertension

Low risk
  • Goal = the ESC/ERS low-risk profile: RA < 8 mmHg, CI ≥ 2.5, SvO₂ > 65%, SVI > 38 mL/m²
  • Falling PVR and RA with a rising CO = therapy working
  • mPAP > 20 defines PH; PAH = mPAP > 20 + PAWP ≤ 15 + PVR > 2 WU
  • Vasoreactive: mPAP drops ≥ 10 to ≤ 40 mmHg with unchanged or higher CO
  • Never pause continuous prostacyclin; dedicated line

The math

  • Cardiac indexCI = CO ÷ BSA
  • Mean arterial pressureMAP = (SBP + 2·DBP) ÷ 3
  • Systemic vascular resistanceSVR = (MAP − RA) ÷ CO × 80
  • Pulmonary vascular resistancePVR = (mPAP − PAWP) ÷ CO
  • Transpulmonary gradientTPG = mPAP − PAWP
  • PA pulsatility indexPAPi = (PAS − PAD) ÷ RA

PVR in Wood units; multiply by 80 for dyn·s·cm⁻⁵.

Scope pending

Wedge (PAWP) and manual thermodilution CO steps are hidden until PACU scope is confirmed. Per SCU 10-408, PAWP on stepdown is performed by the provider, and vasoactives never run through the blue injectate port.

Wedge procedure · Philips IntelliVue

Only with a provider order and a documented full line of data. Per SCU 10-408.

Inflate

  1. Supine, HOB 0–45°. Confirm PA waveform.
  2. Monitoring Procedures → Wedge. PAWP parameter on.
  3. Note prior balloon volume. Fill syringe ≤ 1.5 mL air. Red lines aligned.
  4. At "Ready for wedge procedure," inflate slowly — stop the moment the trace changes.

Measure & deflate

  1. After ~12 sec the trace freezes: deflate passively.
  2. Edit Wedge → cursor to the mean at end-expiration → Confirm.
  3. Syringe off, expel air, reattach and lock.
  4. Confirm PA waveform returns. Document balloon volume.
Handoff
07

Handoff to 6EW

CORE

Bedside SBAR per CP 5-101, with I-TRACE verification of every drip. Use the SBAR MPage in KeckCare.

S

Situation

  • Post-RHC, retained PAC for optimization
  • Indication: HF / transplant-LVAD eval / PH
  • Current rhythm, BP, SpO₂, mental status
  • Any events in PACU
B

Background

  • Access site & side (IJ / subclavian)
  • cm at introducer and balloon volume
  • Cath-lab numbers: RA, PA, PAWP, CO/CI, SvO₂, PVR
  • Anticoagulation given; vasoreactivity drugs used
A

Assessment

  • PA waveform confirmed; last square wave result
  • Time last leveled and zeroed
  • Current PAP / CVP; trend vs cath lab
  • Site, lungs, drips by lumen (I-TRACE)
R

Recommendation

  • Hemodynamic targets and who titrates
  • Pending: labs, CXR, repeat CO/SvO₂
  • Who to call for waveform changes
  • Planned duration of the PAC

Before the patient leaves PACU

  • PA waveform confirmed on distal channel; cm marking unchanged
  • Balloon deflated, syringe attached and locked
  • Dual-channel strip (ECG + PA/RA) saved in the EHR with end-expiratory points marked
  • Flush bag has volume; pressure bag at 300 mmHg
  • Occlusive dressing labeled; no tape on the sleeve
  • All connections luer-lock; red Curos on pressure ports, green Curos on infusion claves
  • Transport monitor displaying the PA waveform with alarms on
  • Transducers secured to transport at phlebostatic level — re-level on arrival
Requirement · SCU 10-408

The PA waveform is monitored continuously, including during transport. PA alarms are never turned off.

Good to know for 6EW

On stepdown, when the PAC is discontinued, the catheter and introducer come out together — no SLIC or obturator cap is placed on stepdown. Transducer, flush bag, tubing, and CO set change every 96 hours.
